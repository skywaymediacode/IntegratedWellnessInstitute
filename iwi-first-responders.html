The Integrated Wellness Institute was founded by two doctorate-level clinicians with one shared conviction: resilience is a capability, not a trait. Like any capability, it can be trained, practiced, and reinforced.

Our programs are grounded in evidence-based practices, designed for the operational realities of first responders, and engineered for adoption, not just delivery. The PEACE Resilience Model

Methodology

A resilience model built specifically for first responders and uniquely aligned with trauma treatment. Five evidence-based components build resilience as a daily, repeatable practice, refined through years of working directly with those who operate under chronic stress and traumatic exposure.

  1. P Psychoeducation on Trauma Identify the impact of trauma on the mind and body, and why understanding the mechanism is the first step toward managing it.
  2. E Expand Awareness Build emotional and environmental awareness so personnel can read their own state, and the situation, before either escalates.
  3. A Activate Healthy Habits Practical routines that meet needs across every level of wellbeing: physical, cognitive, emotional, and relational.
  4. C Coping with Acute Stress Evidence-based techniques personnel can use in the moment, on scene, on shift, not just in a quiet room afterward.
  5. E Establish a Plan An individualized plan personnel actually return to, for building resilience and maintaining wellbeing through a long career.

Why It Matters

The Case

Today’s organizations face increasing demands related to workforce wellness, employee engagement, recruitment, retention, leadership development, and organizational culture. Investing in resilience, wellness, and peer support helps create environments where individuals and teams can perform at their best while navigating the challenges of high-demand professions.

The goal. By equipping individuals, teams, and leaders with practical skills and support, organizations can foster healthier workplaces where people feel connected, valued, and prepared to thrive, both professionally and personally.

  • Employee well-being and quality of life
  • Resilience, adaptability, and stress management
  • Healthy workplace culture and psychological safety
  • Strong communication and team cohesion
  • Peer support and help-seeking behaviors
  • Leadership effectiveness and workforce readiness
  • Employee engagement and job satisfaction
  • Recruitment, retention, and workforce sustainability
  • Organizational health and long-term success
